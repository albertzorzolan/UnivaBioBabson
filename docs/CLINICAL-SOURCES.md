# Kinwise clinical knowledge base: sources, coverage and method

Knowledge base version **2026.10.2** · generated by `python3 -I kb-src/build_kb.py` · this file is generated
from the same inputs as `src/kb/data/*.json` (edit `kb-src/CLINICAL-SOURCES.template.md`, not this file).

> **Status.** The knowledge base was compiled from published criteria and cross-checked by automated research
> and an adversarial fact-check. **It has not been reviewed by a licensed clinician or pharmacist.** It is a
> hackathon prototype for fictional demo data, not a medical device and not clinical decision support for
> treatment. It does not check doses, allergies or every interaction, and a medicine that is not flagged is not
> thereby shown to be free of risk. Every finding Kinwise shows is a question for the clinician or pharmacist.

## 3. Method

1. **Primary criteria, read in full.** AGS Beers Criteria® 2023 tables were pulled from the PMC full text
   (PMC12478568) and compared line by line with the final journal PDF (ratings, thresholds and Table 7 membership:
   0 mismatches in an automated re-parse). The ACB 2012 card, CDC STEADI / STEADI-Rx, STOPPFall (Seppälä 2021),
   ThinkCascades (McCarthy 2022), the PIPC list (Rochon 2025) and the primary cascade studies were read from the
   publisher or PubMed records listed above (`kb-src/research/*.json`, `*-notes.md`).
2. **Adversarial fact-check.** A second research pass tried to disprove each claim (wrong year, wrong table,
   wrong rating, brand mapped to the wrong ingredient); corrections are recorded in the research notes
   (for example Procardia/Adalat removed from the immediate-release nifedipine criterion, Unisom SleepTabs =
   doxylamine vs SleepGels = diphenhydramine, Zantac 360 = famotidine).
3. **Kinwise wording.** All rationale text is Kinwise's own plain-language paraphrase (no AGS sentences are
   reproduced; the research step checked for 8-word verbatim overlap). Drug names, classes, thresholds and AGS
   quality/strength ratings are recorded as published; `build_kb.py` re-checks every rating against
   `research/beers-2023.json`.
4. **Codes and names.** Ingredient RxCUIs come from RxNorm (RxNav API); every brand name was checked against
   RxNorm brand-to-ingredient relations or a DailyMed label title. 425 of 427 medicines carry an RxNorm ingredient RxCUI; the rest have none on purpose (insulin, multivitamin).
5. **Build-time validation** (`build_kb.py`, run on every build; the build refuses to write on any problem):
   every class, drug, combination ingredient, selector and source id resolves; no name maps to two meanings;
   ACB and Table 7 drugs exist; Beers ratings match the research file; every drug a Beers rule names is named in
   that rule's row of the research file (this caught methyldopa, which AGS moved to Table 8 in 2023); a cascade
   cites ThinkCascades only if it is one of the tool's 9 retained cascades and PIPC only if it is on the PIPC list
   (`research/cascades.json`); route and dose entries refine an existing medicine and never carry its Table 7
   class; no banned wording (SPEC §3: causal
   wording, "safe", "dangerous", "urgent", "diagnosis", "stop taking", "risk score"); every question ends with a
   question mark; rationale is one sentence; no abbreviation that would split a sentence.
6. **Tests.** `src/kb/kb.test.ts` (integrity, record shapes, published values, a 150-row name-matching table,
   search ranking, language rules), `src/kb/kb.review.test.ts` (the scope corrections run end to end through
   `matchDrug` and the review engine), `src/engine/meds/seed.review.test.ts` (the SPEC §2 seed acceptance test, run against this KB)
   and the independent evaluation suite below.

## 4. Independent evaluation

`eval/med-cases.json` was written from the published criteria by a separate author **without reading the engine
or this knowledge base** (see `eval/MED-CASES-METHOD.md`). Each case types medicine names the way a family would
("Norco 5/325", "Tylenol PM", "Lorazapam 1 mg"), runs them through `matchDrug` and the review engine, and checks
findings, ACB totals, fall-risk lists and negatives.

Last run (2026-10-11, KB 2026.10.2): **57/57 cases and 295/295 expectations passed** (0 precondition failures). Run it with `npx vitest run eval/meds.eval.test.ts`; results are written to `eval/results/meds-eval.json`.

**Kinwise operational choices** (where AGS gives a concept rather than a number): PPI "more than 8 weeks" = taken
56 days or more; chronic NSAID use = 90 days or more; warfarin "avoid starting" = started within the last 90 days;
nitrofurantoin long-term = 30 days or more; Table 6 "≤ 80" and "15–50 mL/min" are applied as "< 80" and "< 50";
baclofen's eGFR < 60 is compared with the recorded CrCl; the RAS / potassium-sparing (Table 5) and TMP-SMX
(Table 4) kidney rows apply when chronic kidney disease is recorded or CrCl < 60; indication exceptions
(antipsychotics for schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, benzodiazepines for seizures, amiodarone with heart
failure, aspirin for secondary prevention, testosterone for hypogonadism) are read from the medicine's recorded
reason, and when no reason is recorded the finding says it "depends on why it is taken". For aspirin, only
history phrases ("after", "had a", "history of", "stent", "TIA", ...) mark secondary prevention, so the commonest
primary-prevention reason, "to prevent a heart attack", still matches; amiodarone's first-line criterion applies
when the reason mentions atrial fibrillation (or is blank). The Table 4 and Table 6 trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
rows name both ingredients (the text reads "the sulfamethoxazole and trimethoprim in Bactrim") and also match
trimethoprim alone, which is the component that raises potassium; the Table 5 warfarin and phenytoin rows key on
sulfamethoxazole (the CYP2C9 inhibitor).

**Route and dose.** Several criteria are scoped by route or dose: oral NSAIDs (Table 2), systemic estrogen
("low-dose intravaginal estrogen ... acceptable", Tables 2 and 3), atropine excluding ophthalmic use, systemic
corticosteroids (Table 3 footnote: "excludes inhaled and topical forms"), oral diphenhydramine, and doxepin
> 6 mg/day. Kinwise models these as separate entries: diclofenac on the skin (Voltaren gel, Pennsaid, Flector),
low-dose vaginal estradiol and conjugated estrogens (Vagifem, Estring, Imvexxy, Estrace or Premarin cream),
atropine, ketorolac, prednisolone and dexamethasone eye drops, hydrocortisone and diphenhydramine on the skin,
and low-dose doxepin (Silenor, or doxepin typed with a strength of 6 mg or less). The name matcher selects them
from brand names or route words in the typed name ("gel", "cream", "eye drops", "vaginal"), and sends a brand back
to the oral entry when the text says so ("Voltaren 75 mg tablets"). They share the base ingredient's RxCUI and
score 0 on the ACB scale (the card scores medicines taken by mouth or injection at usual doses). Lomotil's
0.025 mg of atropine is kept under the GI-antispasmodic criterion, as Beers lists atropine without a dose limit.

## 6. Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) scale

100 medicines carry an ACB score (score 3: 43, score 2: 12, score 1: 45), transcribed from the 2012 card; the card's 'reviewed but not added' drugs (e.g. gabapentin) score 0. Scores are dose-independent; combination products score each ingredient; an ingredient taken in two
products counts once. A total of **3 or more** is the published review threshold ("elevated"); Kinwise labels 5 or
more as "high" for display only (not a published cut-off). The ACB scale is © Regenstrief Institute; Kinwise uses
it non-commercially with attribution.

## 9. What to watch for (SPEC §12)

Beers "avoid" medicines are implicated in only about 1–2% of emergency visits for medication harm in older adults;
anticoagulants, diabetes medicines, antiplatelets and opioids account for most (Budnitz 2011, 2021; Shehab 2016).
These rules are not problem flags: they list what is worth noticing and link it to the diary.

## 10. Known effects (diary links)

Each medicine lists the diary measures (dizziness, drowsiness, confusion, falls, near-falls, ankle swelling, low
energy, poor sleep, poor appetite) that appear in its FDA label (adverse reactions, warnings, geriatric use;
openFDA / DailyMed, `research/symptom-adr-map.json`) or, for supplements, MedlinePlus. Class-level effects are
listed in `EFFECTS_BY_CLASS` in `build_kb.py`; drug-level additions and removals are in the drug sources (for
example meclizine does not list dizziness because it is taken for dizziness; metformin lists no diary effect
because its label's GI effects are not a diary measure). Medicines in a FRID class also list falls and near-falls.
Effects are used only to link a recorded symptom to a finding ("Could X be contributing to the dizziness recorded…?")
and to mark timeline signals as a "known possible effect"; they are never used to say a medicine caused anything.

## 12. Limitations

- Not reviewed by a licensed clinician; class membership beyond the examples AGS names is Kinwise's expansion
  (checked against FDA Established Pharmacologic Class).
- Dose and formulation are machine-checked only where listed above (route entries, low-dose doxepin); immediate vs
  extended release, other dose thresholds and regimen are not.
- Indication exceptions depend on the free-text reason a family records.
- ACB, FRID and cascade lists are population-level associations; a flag is a reason to ask, not evidence of harm
  in the person.
- Drug–drug interactions are limited to AGS Beers Table 5 (plus the NSAID-with-antithrombotic part of Table 2);
  this is not a full interaction checker.
- "AGS Beers Criteria®" is a registered trademark of the American Geriatrics Society; Kinwise is not affiliated
  with or endorsed by AGS.

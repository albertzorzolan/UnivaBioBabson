#### About the challenge

UnivaBio is a global student competition open to students aged 13+ hosted collaboratively by **BioCataalysis** and **UnivaDev** in support of innovation at the intersection of healthcare and technology.

Theme: Artificial Intelligence for Human Health

This event specifically brings together young innovators from worldwide to explore AI technology’s role in human health. Working individually or in teams, participants will design and build a meaningful technical product that helps people detect illness earlier, understand it better, or live with it more easily.

Your project can take almost any form: an app, a website, a simulation, a system, or an algorithm. This can be an AI EdTech app teaching about specific health concepts, a classification model trained on imaging datasets, or an innovation that one wants to propose that has significant medical implications.

You are encouraged to use AI assistants while building. We want the technical barrier to be low enough that beginners with ambitious ideas can actually ship them. What matters is that you understand what you built and can explain it. Be prepared to walk judges through your own code.

Submission Requirements

*   Project: Can be a website, app, or other coding prototypes that have user interaction capabilities.

*   Demo Video: Video explaining the purpose of the project, showcasing project features, and demonstrating how users interact with the prototype.

*   One Page Project Description in PDF format: A page describing the project purpose and key features.

*   Github Repository / Code PDF: PDF document showcasing all code developed towards the creation of the project.

*   **Idea & Innovation**  
 Did the proposal address a real problem within the theme? Was the idea innovative? Can this be meaningfully different from other things that exist already? 
*   **Implementation**  
 Does the solution work? Did the team achieve everything they wanted? How technically challenging/impressive was the implementation? 
*   **Health Impact & Rigor**  
 Who is this for, and would it actually help them? Is there great potential? 
*   **Design & Usability**  
 Did the team put thought into the user experience? How well designed is the interface? 
*   **Presentation**  
 Is the problem clearly defined? Does the demo show the thing actually working? Is the team honest about what's finished versus planned?
